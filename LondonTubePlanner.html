About the Planner

An unofficial helper for getting around London by Tube: all 11 London Underground lines plus the Elizabeth line. Enter your location by GPS, address or station, choose a destination, and get a step-by-step guide covering which line and platform direction to take, which trains to let pass, where to change, and what the fare comes to.

How route finding works

Location → Best Station
The Haversine formula gives the distance from your GPS position or address to every station. The planner tries the nearest few (up to 5 within walking range) and picks whichever gets you there soonest once walking time is counted, so a slightly further station on the right line beats an extra change.
Branches and through trains
Each service pattern is its own chain of stops: the Northern line's Morden–Edgware via Bank, Battersea–High Barnet via Charing Cross, the District's Edgware Road–Wimbledon, and so on, checked against TfL's timetables. Only trains that run all day count as through trains, so the planner never sends you on one that doesn't exist and tells you where to change instead: at Kennington for the Charing Cross branch, Earl's Court for Richmond, Baker Street for Watford trains, Hatton Cross for Terminal 4.
Dijkstra's Shortest Path
Changing trains has a real cost: the walk between the two lines' platforms (positions from OpenStreetMap, so long walks like Bank or Green Park count) plus an average wait, longer on branches with few trains. Official out-of-station interchanges such as Euston ⇄ Euston Square and the two Hammersmith stations are included. Dijkstra's algorithm then finds the quickest journey, with a small extra penalty per change to prefer direct routes.
Line and Direction
London platforms are signed by direction (Northbound, Eastbound and so on; Inner and Outer Rail on the western side of the Circle line and the Central line's Hainault loop) and each train shows its destination. Every step gives both, following TfL's own names per section of line, and warns you when trains on the same platform go somewhere else, such as Bakerloo trains ending at Queen's Park.

Fares

Adult pay as you go fares with contactless or Oyster, from 1 March 2026. Peak is Monday to Friday 06:30–09:30 and 16:00–19:00 (except public holidays), but travel into Zone 1 from outside it in the evening peak is charged off-peak. You pay for every zone you pass through; boundary stations count as whichever zone is cheaper. Tube journeys to Heathrow through Zone 1 are always charged the peak fare; Elizabeth line fares to Heathrow are higher (£15.50 from central London), and travel between the terminals and Hatton Cross is free. A few short Zone 1 ⇄ Zone 2 trips (such as Euston to Camden Town) are charged the Zone 1 fare, so the planner may show up to 50p more for those. Daily caps were frozen for 2026.

Good to know

Hours
Trains run from about 05:00 until shortly after midnight (later starts on Sundays). The Waterloo & City line runs Monday to Friday only. Last trains vary by line and station, so check the times posted at the station if you're travelling late.
Night Tube
On Friday and Saturday nights trains keep running every 10–20 minutes on the Victoria and Jubilee lines, the Central line between Ealing Broadway and Hainault or Loughton, the Northern line's Charing Cross branch (Morden to Edgware and High Barnet) and the Piccadilly line between Cockfosters and Heathrow Terminal 5. At those times the planner only uses those lines.
What's covered
All 11 Underground lines, and the Elizabeth line within London's fare zones: Heathrow and West Drayton to Abbey Wood and Harold Wood (trains continue to Reading, Maidenhead and Shenfield, which have their own fares). Not covered: the DLR, London Overground, trams, National Rail, and the District line's Kensington (Olympia) shuttle, which mostly runs at weekends.
Data and accuracy
Station positions come from OpenStreetMap; service patterns, fare zones, platform directions and interchanges were checked against TfL's open data in October 2026. Distances are straight lines scaled to each line's official length, and times come from a model fitted to TfL's timetabled stop-to-stop times, so journey times and fares are estimates. Always follow in-station signs and check TfL for engineering works.
